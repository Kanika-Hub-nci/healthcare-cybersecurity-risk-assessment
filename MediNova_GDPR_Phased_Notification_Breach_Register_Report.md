## 1. Executive Summary

This report documents a fictional phased personal data breach notification process at MediNova, a simulated healthcare organisation.

The scenario involves unauthorised access to patient records through a former employee's administrator account.

Following an initial GDPR Article 33 notification estimating that approximately 50 patients may have been affected, further investigation established that approximately 180 patients were affected.

The investigation also identified an additional category of sensitive personal data.

These developments require the organisation to update its breach register, coordinate supplementary information for the competent supervisory authority without undue further delay, and reassess the potential consequences for affected individuals.

**Assessment Outcome:** Material new findings identified — supplementary notification recommended.

**Article 34 Status:** Reassessment pending.

No real personal data breach, regulatory submission or patient information is represented.

## 5. GDPR Article 33(4) — Phased Notification

GDPR Article 33(4) permits the information required by Article 33(3) to be provided in phases where it is not possible to provide all the information at the same time.

In this scenario, the controller has already submitted an initial notification.

The new findings should be supplied to the competent supervisory authority without undue further delay.

The supplementary submission should clearly distinguish:

- Information previously reported.
- Newly established facts.
- Corrections to earlier estimates.
- Information still under investigation.
- Updated likely consequences.
- Additional remedial measures.

The supplementary notification should not silently replace or erase the original notification record.

## 10. Article 34 — Reassessment of Communication Obligations

GDPR Article 34 generally requires communication to affected individuals without undue delay where a personal data breach is likely to result in a high risk to their rights and freedoms, subject to applicable exceptions.

The updated MediNova assessment should consider:

- The sensitivity of the additional personal data category.
- The number and circumstances of affected individuals.
- The possible consequences of unauthorised access.
- The potential for discrimination, identity misuse, financial harm or other adverse effects.
- The effectiveness of containment and protective measures.
- Whether any Article 34(3) exception applies.

**Communication to Affected Individuals:** Not yet determined.

The increase in affected individuals and identification of additional sensitive information warrant reassessment, but do not automatically establish the final Article 34 decision.

## 15. Closure Criteria

Before recommending administrative closure of the breach management record, the organisation should verify that:

1. The investigation has reached an appropriately documented conclusion.
2. Relevant Article 33 notification obligations have been addressed.
3. Article 34 obligations have been assessed and acted upon where required.
4. The breach register contains the required facts, effects and remedial actions.
5. Evidence and decision records are retained.
6. Outstanding corrective actions have accountable owners and monitoring arrangements.
7. Closure is authorised under the organisation's approved process.

Closure of the breach management record does not automatically mean that every related cybersecurity risk or control finding has been remediated.

## 16. GRC Recommendations

The GRC Analyst should recommend:

1. Preserving the original notification and supporting evidence.
2. Recording the revised affected population and newly identified data category.
3. Coordinating supplementary information without undue further delay.
4. Reassessing the potential risk and high risk to affected individuals.
5. Maintaining a complete, versioned breach register.
6. Documenting management decisions and regulatory correspondence.
7. Tracking remediation and control effectiveness.
8. Keeping the breach record open until applicable closure requirements are satisfied.

**Final Recommendation: UPDATE NOTIFICATION, REASSESS INDIVIDUAL RISK AND MAINTAIN AN AUDITABLE BREACH REGISTER.**

## 17. Conclusion

The fictional MediNova scenario demonstrates why GDPR breach management requires continuous assessment rather than a single notification event.

As new information becomes available, the controller must update its understanding of the breach, maintain appropriate documentation and meet applicable notification obligations.

The revised finding of approximately 180 affected patients and the identification of additional sensitive personal data warrant supplementary regulatory information and renewed assessment of Article 34 obligations.
