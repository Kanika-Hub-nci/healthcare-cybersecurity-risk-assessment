## 1. Executive Summary

This report documents a fictional GDPR personal data breach assessment involving unauthorised access to patient records at MediNova.

The incident originated from an employee offboarding control weakness in which a former employee's administrator account remained active.

Within the simulated investigation, unauthorised access to patient personal data has been confirmed.

The exact number of affected individuals, categories of records involved and whether information was exported remain under investigation.

The fictional privacy assessment concludes that the breach is likely to result in a risk to individuals' rights and freedoms.

Under these scenario assumptions, notification to the competent supervisory authority is required in accordance with GDPR Article 33.

The organisation must separately assess whether the breach is likely to result in a high risk to individuals, requiring communication under Article 34, subject to applicable exceptions.

**Assessment Outcome:** Article 33 notification required under the scenario assumptions; Article 34 decision pending.

No actual regulatory notification, management approval or real-world breach is represented.

## 3. Personal Data Breach Classification

GDPR Article 4(12) defines a personal data breach as a breach of security leading to accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorised disclosure of, or access to personal data.

In the fictional MediNova scenario, unauthorised access to patient personal data has been established.

**Personal Data Breach Classification:** Confirmed within the training scenario.

**Breach Type:** Confidentiality breach.

**Data Export:** Not established.

**Affected Population:** Under investigation.

## 5. Article 33 — Supervisory Authority Notification Assessment

## 6. Phased Notification

GDPR Article 33(4) allows information to be provided in phases where it is not possible to provide all required information at the same time.

In this scenario, the organisation should not delay a required initial notification solely because the exact number of affected patients is unknown.

The initial notification should include the information available at the time, with further details supplied without undue additional delay.
