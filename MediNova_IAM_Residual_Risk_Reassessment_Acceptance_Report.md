## 1. Executive Summary

This report documents a fictional residual risk reassessment and proposed management risk acceptance assessment for MediNova's employee offboarding access control.

An earlier operating effectiveness test identified two failures among ten employee departures.

Following reported corrective action implementation, a retest of twelve fictional departures recorded eleven passing cases and one failure.

The remaining exception involved account deactivation after 30 hours, exceeding MediNova's internal 24-hour requirement.

Management has proposed considering acceptance of the remaining risk because further automation may require additional funding.

However, the residual risk has not yet been formally reassessed, the effectiveness of potential compensating controls has not been verified and no authorised risk acceptance decision has been recorded.

**Assessment Outcome:** Risk acceptance cannot be treated as approved.

**GRC Recommendation:** Complete residual risk reassessment, evaluate treatment alternatives and submit any justified acceptance request through the authorised governance process.

GAP-001 remains open.

## 2. Purpose and Scope

The purpose of this assessment is to:

1. Evaluate the current status of the IAM offboarding risk.
2. Identify evidence needed for residual risk reassessment.
3. Examine available treatment options.
4. Assess the proposed business justification for risk acceptance.
5. Identify appropriate approval and monitoring requirements.
6. Clarify the relationship between risk acceptance and audit finding closure.

## 4. Risk Statement

If employee accounts are not disabled within the approved timeframe after departure, former employees or other unauthorised persons may retain access to organisational systems.

This may result in unauthorised access, confidentiality breaches, misuse of privileged permissions or other security incidents.

The likelihood and impact require assessment using the organisation's approved risk methodology.

## 5. Inherent and Residual Risk

### Inherent Risk

The level of risk considered before the effects of relevant controls and treatments.

### Residual Risk

The level of risk remaining after taking existing controls and treatments into account.

### Current Assessment

A reliable residual risk rating cannot be assigned solely from the illustrative retest pass rate.

The risk owner must consider:

- Account privileges.
- Accessible systems and information.
- Deactivation delays.
- Monitoring coverage.
- Verified compensating controls.
- Incident history.
- Potential impact.
- Likelihood of misuse.

**Residual Risk Rating:** Pending reassessment.

## 6. Available Risk Treatment Options

| Option | Description | Consideration |
|---|---|---|
| Risk reduction | Improve offboarding automation, monitoring or workflow controls | Further implementation and validation may be required |
| Risk avoidance | Remove or redesign activities that create the exposure | May not be operationally feasible |
| Risk sharing | Transfer or share certain financial consequences where appropriate | Does not remove accountability or necessarily reduce security exposure |
| Risk acceptance | Authorised decision to retain residual risk | Requires documented justification and governance |

The risk owner should assess the suitability of available options before seeking acceptance.

## 10. Acceptance Decision Criteria

Before considering approval, the authorised decision-maker should receive:

1. A current risk assessment.
2. The relevant control testing results.
3. Investigation findings for the remaining exception.
4. Evidence supporting existing and compensating controls.
5. Available treatment options.
6. Cost and feasibility considerations.
7. A proposed acceptance period.
8. Monitoring and reporting arrangements.
9. Escalation and review triggers.
10. An assessment of applicable legal, regulatory and contractual obligations.

An internal risk acceptance decision cannot waive applicable legal requirements.

## 12. Risk Acceptance Monitoring Plan

If an acceptance request is later approved, the organisation should establish proportionate monitoring.

Potential monitoring activities include:

- Review of overdue account deactivations.
- Monitoring of privileged accounts.
- Review of offboarding exceptions.
- Periodic assessment of compensating controls.
- Tracking of additional remediation opportunities.
- Review of changes to systems, data or permissions.
- Escalation when approved risk conditions are exceeded.

Monitoring arrangements should be defined by the authorised approval.

## 13. Reassessment and Escalation Triggers

Potential triggers include:

1. A new unauthorised access incident.
2. Repeated offboarding failures.
3. Increased privileged access exposure.
4. Failure of compensating controls.
5. Material changes to affected systems or data.
6. Changes to legal or regulatory requirements.
7. Expiry of an approved acceptance.
8. Evidence that the current risk assessment is no longer valid.

Material changes should prompt reassessment rather than automatic continuation of an earlier decision.

## 14. Relationship to GAP-001

Risk acceptance and audit finding closure are separate governance decisions.

An authorised acceptance of residual risk does not prove that corrective actions were effective.

## 18. Conclusion

The fictional MediNova scenario demonstrates that improved control performance does not eliminate the need for residual risk assessment.

Although corrective action retesting showed improvement, one account deactivation failure remained.

Management may consider accepting the residual risk, but such a decision requires appropriate evidence, business justification and authorised approval.

Risk acceptance must not be confused with corrective action effectiveness or audit finding closure.
