## 1. Executive Summary

This report documents a fictional security incident involving the use of a former employee's account after their employment ended at MediNova.

For this training scenario, the Incident Response Team has completed the investigation, confirmed containment and recovery, documented its response activities and authorised incident closure.

However, the underlying employee offboarding weakness has not yet been fully remediated.

**Important:** This report represents a simulated incident closure. No real security investigation, forensic examination or management approval occurred.

## 2. Incident Description

A former employee's account was identified as having been used after the employee's departure.

This raised concerns about potential unauthorised access to sensitive patient information and weaknesses in identity lifecycle management.

The scenario does not establish whether patient information was disclosed, altered or exfiltrated.

## 5. Root Cause Analysis

**Suspected Root Cause:** Inadequate or inconsistently implemented HR–IT employee offboarding procedures.

Possible contributing factors include:

- Delayed communication of employee departures.
- Dependence on manual offboarding notifications.
- Lack of clearly assigned account deactivation responsibilities.
- Insufficient reconciliation between HR departure records and active user accounts.

The underlying root cause requires confirmation through investigation records and process evidence.

## 6. Lessons Learned

### Lesson 1 — Employee Offboarding Must Be Reliable

Employee accounts should be disabled or adjusted promptly according to organisational requirements when employment ends.

### Lesson 2 — HR and IT Responsibilities Must Be Clear

The organisation should establish clear ownership for departure notifications and access removal.

### Lesson 3 — Access Reviews Are Important

Periodic comparisons between employee records and active accounts can help identify unnecessary access.

### Lesson 4 — Incident Evidence Must Be Preserved

Relevant authentication and access logs should be retained appropriately to support investigation and accountability.

### Lesson 5 — Incident Closure Does Not Mean Risk Closure

Long-term corrective actions must continue to be monitored after the immediate incident is resolved.

## 11. Conclusion

This simulated incident demonstrates the importance of maintaining effective identity lifecycle management and employee offboarding controls.

Although the fictional incident response has concluded, the underlying risk remains open until the organisation implements and verifies effective corrective actions.

The key lesson is that incident closure and risk remediation are separate but connected governance activities.
