## 1. Executive Summary

This report documents a fictional GDPR Article 34 notification delivery assurance review at MediNova, a simulated healthcare organisation.

Following a personal data breach involving unauthorised access to patient records, the organisation determined that communication to affected individuals was required.

The fictional communications team dispatched notifications to 180 affected patients.

Delivery records indicate that 165 messages were delivered successfully, while 15 messages were returned as undeliverable.

The privacy team has not yet assessed appropriate alternative communication methods for the 15 unresolved cases.

Although the recorded delivery rate is approximately 91.7%, the outstanding delivery failures require further assessment and documented follow-up.

**Assessment Outcome:** Closure not recommended.

**Required Action:** Escalate unresolved notification failures, assess appropriate follow-up methods, preserve supporting evidence and obtain an authorised closure decision.

No actual patient communications or regulatory activities are represented.

## 2. Background

The underlying fictional incident involved unauthorised access to patient records through a former employee's administrator account.

The privacy team assessed that the breach was likely to result in a high risk to affected individuals.

No applicable Article 34 exception was established.

The organisation therefore proceeded with an illustrative individual communication process.

The present review focuses specifically on delivery assurance and closure readiness.

It does not independently reassess the original breach classification.

## 4. GDPR Requirements

### Article 34 — Communication to Affected Individuals

Where a personal data breach is likely to result in a high risk to individuals' rights and freedoms, GDPR Article 34 generally requires the controller to communicate the breach to affected individuals without undue delay, subject to applicable exceptions.

The communication must meet the content requirements of Article 34(2).

### Article 5(2) — Accountability

The controller is responsible for compliance with the principles in Article 5(1) and must be able to demonstrate compliance.

### Article 24 — Controller Responsibility

The controller must implement appropriate technical and organisational measures to ensure and demonstrate that processing complies with the GDPR.

### Article 33(5) — Breach Documentation

The controller must document personal data breaches, including the facts, effects and remedial action taken.

### Practical Assurance Implication

The GDPR does not establish a universal requirement to prove that every affected individual opened or read a communication.

However, delivery failures should be assessed and handled appropriately to support fulfilment of the communication obligation.

## 17. Recommended Closure Criteria

Closure should be considered only when:

1. Relevant communication records have been reviewed.
2. Delivery exceptions have been appropriately investigated.
3. The privacy team has assessed necessary follow-up.
4. Appropriate alternative communications have been undertaken or a documented, defensible decision explains why further steps are not required.
5. Supporting evidence is retained.
6. Outstanding obligations have been addressed.
7. The breach register has been updated.
8. The authorised decision-maker approves closure.

A documented, justified exception resolution may be appropriate even where actual receipt cannot be proved.

Closure must not be based solely on the percentage of messages successfully delivered.
