# MediNova Healthcare — Cybersecurity GRC Portfolio

**Governance | Risk Management | ISO 27001 | IAM | Audit Assurance | GDPR**

A completed, scenario-based cybersecurity Governance, Risk and Compliance (GRC) portfolio demonstrating how security risks, control gaps, audit findings, incidents and management decisions can be documented and assessed in a fictional healthcare organisation.

**Project Type:** Independent educational simulation  
**Status:** Completed  
**Focus:** Junior Cybersecurity GRC, IT Risk and Compliance Analyst skills

---

## Project Overview

MediNova Healthcare is a fictional organisation that handles sensitive patient information.

This portfolio demonstrates an integrated GRC workflow, from identifying security weaknesses through risk assessment, treatment planning, control testing, incident governance and management reporting.

The project uses simulated scenarios, illustrative test results and structured working documents.

No production systems, real patient data or actual company audit evidence were used.

## Key Skills Demonstrated

- ISO/IEC 27001:2022 gap assessment and control mapping
- Cybersecurity risk identification and treatment planning
- Risk registers, risk monitoring and management dashboards
- Identity and Access Management (IAM) assurance
- Audit evidence requirements and exception tracking
- Root cause analysis and corrective action validation
- Control operating effectiveness testing and retesting
- Residual risk reassessment and risk acceptance governance
- Incident escalation and GDPR breach assessment
- Management reporting and audit finding closure assessment

## Featured Case Study — Employee Offboarding Risk

### Business Problem

A fictional former employee account remained active after the employee's departure, creating a risk of unauthorised access.

### GRC Approach

The project documents:

1. Identification of the control gap (GAP-001).
2. Assessment of the associated IAM risk (RISK-IAM-001).
3. Review of corrective action and audit evidence requirements.
4. Operating effectiveness testing.
5. Investigation of failed control samples.
6. Corrective action retesting.
7. Residual risk reassessment.
8. Conditional risk acceptance and monitoring requirements.

### Illustrative Test Results

| Metric | Initial Test | Retest |
|---|---:|---:|
| Samples tested | 10 | 12 |
| Passed | 8 | 11 |
| Failed | 2 | 1 |
| Sample pass rate | 80% | 91.7% |

The fictional results showed improved sample performance but one remaining control failure.

**GRC Conclusion:** The audit finding remains open because improved performance and conditional risk acceptance do not independently establish successful remediation.

## Featured Case Study — GDPR Breach Governance

A separate connected simulation examines unauthorised access to patient records and the governance processes associated with GDPR breach assessment.

The project covers:

- Personal data breach assessment.
- Article 33 notification decision-making.
- Supplementary notification as additional information becomes available.
- Article 34 communication to affected individuals.
- Delivery exceptions and follow-up.
- Communication workstream closure.
- Continuing oversight of the underlying IAM weakness.

The scenario illustrates why regulatory communication closure does not automatically establish security incident closure or audit finding remediation.

## Key Learning Outcomes

This project strengthened my ability to:

- Translate a technical weakness into a business risk.
- Identify the evidence needed to test a control.
- Distinguish control design from operating effectiveness.
- Document and investigate audit exceptions.
- Assess corrective action closure readiness.
- Explain residual risk and management acceptance.
- Prepare clear management-level GRC reports.
- Recognise when evidence is insufficient to support a conclusion.

## Project Limitations

This is an independent educational portfolio.

All scenarios, organisations, employees, security events, audit findings, risk decisions and test results are fictional.

The work does not represent a real-world audit engagement, independent assurance opinion, penetration test, ISO 27001 certification or verified GDPR compliance.
