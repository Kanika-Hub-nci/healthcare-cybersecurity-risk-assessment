# MediNova Privacy & AI Governance Advisory Project
## Project Brief: AI-Powered Patient Triage Assistant

**Document ID:** PRIV-AI-001  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** 24 October 2026  
**Status:** Independent portfolio simulation  
**Organisation:** MediNova Healthcare (fictional)  
**Prepared by:** Junior Privacy & AI Governance Consultant (simulation)

> **Important limitation:** This is an educational simulation using fictional assumptions. It is not legal advice, a real client engagement, a completed statutory DPIA, or evidence of actual GDPR/EU AI Act compliance. All conclusions must be validated against the real system and processing before being relied upon.

## 1. Executive summary

MediNova Healthcare is considering an AI-powered patient triage assistant to help organise patient-submitted requests and support the prioritisation of clinical review. The proposed system may process personal data, including special-category health data. If the system is poorly designed or overseen, patients could experience privacy harms, unfair prioritisation, delayed care, or decisions they cannot understand or challenge.

This project simulates the early advisory work a junior privacy and AI governance consultant could perform: clarify the proposed use, identify information gaps, scope the assessment, screen for the need for a Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA), and develop a risk-based work plan before making compliance conclusions.

**Initial position:** Do not approve launch based on this brief alone. Confirm the intended purpose, data flows, decision-making role, supplier arrangements, lawful bases, clinical safety controls, and applicable legal obligations first.

## 2. Business scenario

MediNova is a fictional Irish healthcare provider exploring an AI assistant that would review information submitted by patients through a digital channel. It may categorise requests and suggest a priority for review by a qualified healthcare professional.

The project assumes, for assessment purposes only, that:
- Patients may submit symptoms and other health-related information.
- The system may use an external technology provider or hosted service.
- Staff may see an AI-generated category or priority recommendation.
- A human clinical reviewer is intended to remain involved; the precise level of human control has not yet been confirmed.
- The system has not yet been approved for live deployment.

These assumptions are prompts for discovery, not verified facts.

## 3. Project objectives

1. Understand the proposed processing and intended clinical workflow.
2. Identify the personal data, data subjects, purposes, recipients, storage locations, and retention arrangements.
3. Assess whether the processing is necessary and proportionate to the stated purpose.
4. Screen whether a DPIA is required and define the assessment scope.
5. Identify potential risks to patients' rights and freedoms.
6. Recommend safeguards and accountable follow-up actions.
7. Identify GDPR and AI governance questions requiring specialist or legal confirmation.
8. Produce concise, evidence-led advice suitable for discussion with a client.

## 4. Scope

### In scope
- Initial client discovery and engagement scoping.
- Mapping proposed processing activities and data flows.
- Data minimisation, transparency, access, security, retention, and accountability considerations.
- Preliminary DPIA screening and, in later project stages, a structured DPIA.
- Initial analysis of GDPR Article 6 lawful basis and the applicable Article 9 condition for health data.
- Processor/vendor questions, including instructions, subprocessors, data location, retention, and use of data to train models.
- AI governance questions: intended purpose, human oversight, accuracy, bias, explainability, monitoring, accountability, and potential EU AI Act applicability.
- A prioritised action tracker and client-facing recommendations.

### Out of scope
- Clinical validation or medical-device certification.
- Penetration testing or technical security testing.
- A legal opinion or definitive determination of EU AI Act classification.
- A claim that the organisation is GDPR compliant.
- Processing of real patient data.
- Approval to deploy the system.

## 5. Key stakeholders to involve

| Stakeholder | Input needed |
|---|---|
| Project sponsor / senior management | Business objective, risk appetite, launch decision owner |
| Clinical lead | Clinical workflow, safety risks, human review and escalation |
| Data Protection Officer (DPO), if appointed | Independent advice, DPIA oversight and data protection questions |
| IT / security lead | Architecture, access controls, logging, encryption and incident response |
| Product / AI owner | Intended purpose, model behaviour, testing and monitoring |
| Procurement / vendor manager | Supplier terms, subprocessors, service changes and exit arrangements |
| Legal / compliance adviser | Legal basis, sector-specific obligations and unresolved legal questions |

The DPO, where appointed, should be involved appropriately and retain the independence required for the role. The project consultant does not replace the controller's accountability or the DPO's responsibilities.

## 6. Main deliverables

1. Client discovery and scoping report.
2. Processing and data-flow inventory.
3. DPIA screening rationale and DPIA working document.
4. Necessity and proportionality assessment.
5. GDPR lawful-basis analysis (Article 6 and Article 9).
6. Privacy risk register with proposed mitigations and residual-risk review.
7. Initial AI governance applicability and control questions.
8. Prioritised remediation tracker with owners and evidence requirements.
9. Executive summary and client presentation.

## 7. Working method

The assessment will follow an evidence-led sequence:
1. Confirm facts and intended purpose.
2. Map data, people, systems, suppliers and decisions.
3. Identify applicable requirements and unresolved questions.
4. Assess risks to individuals, including likelihood and severity.
5. Consider safeguards and whether they reduce risk sufficiently.
6. Assign owners and deadlines to actions.
7. Document assumptions, limitations and decisions.
8. Revisit the assessment when the system, data, supplier, or intended use changes.

## 8. Initial risks to investigate

- Collection of unnecessary health information.
- Unauthorised access or disclosure of patient data.
- Unclear lawful basis or Article 9 condition.
- Patients not receiving clear information about AI use.
- Incorrect or biased prioritisation leading to delayed clinical review.
- Excessive reliance on AI recommendations.
- Vendor retention or secondary use of patient data, including model training.
- Inadequate retention, deletion, audit logging or incident response.
- Insufficient monitoring after deployment.
- The system's regulatory status or EU AI Act obligations being misunderstood.

These are preliminary risk hypotheses, not confirmed findings.

## 9. Success criteria

The project will be considered complete when:
- Key processing facts and assumptions are documented.
- The DPIA screening decision has a clear rationale.
- Risks to individuals are described in plain language.
- Proposed mitigations have owners and evidence requirements.
- Unresolved high risks and decisions are escalated rather than hidden.
- Recommendations distinguish confirmed facts, assumptions and matters requiring legal or specialist review.
- No statement overclaims compliance, clinical safety, or certification.
