## 1. Executive Summary

This report presents a fictional post-incident governance and closure assurance assessment for MediNova, a simulated healthcare organisation.

The scenario concerns a personal data breach resulting from unauthorised access to patient records through an account associated with a former employee.

The organisation previously identified approximately 180 affected patients and determined that communication to those individuals was required under GDPR Article 34.

Following the communication process, 15 initial delivery failures required additional assessment.

Within this training scenario:

- Ten patients were subsequently contacted through an approved alternative method.
- The remaining five cases received documented privacy assessments and follow-up decisions.
- The relevant communication evidence was reviewed.
- Authorised management approved closure of the Article 34 communication workstream.

However, the employee offboarding control weakness associated with the breach has not yet passed corrective action effectiveness testing.

Consequently, closure of the communication workstream does not justify closure of the underlying cybersecurity risk or audit finding.

**Assessment Outcome:** Article 34 communication workstream closure approved in the simulation.

**Residual Governance Position:** IAM risk and offboarding finding remain open.

## 2. Purpose and Scope

The purpose of this report is to assess the governance requirements associated with closing a GDPR breach communication workstream while maintaining oversight of unresolved security risks.

The assessment covers:

1. Communication closure evidence.
2. Notification exception resolution.
3. Management approval.
4. Breach register documentation.
5. Residual cybersecurity risks.
6. Corrective action effectiveness.
7. Post-incident assurance.
8. Continuing management reporting.

This report does not independently verify technical remediation or establish closure of the underlying security incident.

## 4. Applicable Regulatory and Control Requirements

### 4.1 GDPR Article 5(2) — Accountability

The controller must be responsible for, and able to demonstrate compliance with, the principles set out in Article 5(1).

### 4.2 GDPR Article 24 — Controller Responsibility

The controller must implement appropriate technical and organisational measures to ensure and demonstrate that processing complies with the GDPR.

### 4.3 GDPR Article 33(5) — Breach Documentation

The controller must document personal data breaches, including the facts relating to the breach, its effects and the remedial action taken.

### 4.4 GDPR Article 34 — Communication to Individuals

Where a personal data breach is likely to result in high risk to individuals' rights and freedoms, the controller must generally communicate the breach without undue delay, subject to applicable exceptions.

### 4.5 Relevant ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Controls

- A.5.18 — Access Rights.
- A.5.24 — Information Security Incident Management Planning and Preparation.
- A.5.25 — Assessment and Decision on Information Security Events.

- A.5.28 — Collection of Evidence.

- A.8.16 — Monitoring Activities.

These controls provide a relevant security governance reference. Their implementation and effectiveness have not been independently verified.

## 12. Post-Incident Lessons Learned

The fictional incident highlights several governance lessons.

### Lesson 1 — Offboarding Is a Security Control

Employee departure processes must be coordinated with identity and access management.

### Lesson 2 — Privileged Access Requires Additional Oversight

Administrator accounts can create significant exposure when their ownership and permissions are not appropriately managed.

### Lesson 3 — Regulatory Communication Is Not Remediation

Completing required breach notifications does not eliminate the underlying cause of the incident.

### Lesson 4 — Evidence Supports Closure Decisions

Closure decisions should be based on documented actions, supporting evidence and appropriate authorisation.

### Lesson 5 — Corrective Action Effectiveness Must Be Verified

Implementing a new procedure does not necessarily prove that it works consistently.

These are educational lessons drawn from the simulated scenario, not findings from an actual post-incident investigation.

## 17. Separate Closure Criteria for RISK-IAM-001

The IAM risk should remain subject to risk management until its disposition is supported by an authorised decision.

Potential outcomes may include:

- Continued risk treatment.
- Risk reduction supported by verified controls.
- Formal risk acceptance by an authorised decision-maker, where permitted.
- Risk closure where justified by the organisation's approved criteria.

Communication closure alone is not a valid basis for closing RISK-IAM-001.
