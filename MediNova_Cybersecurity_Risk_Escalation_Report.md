# MediNova — Cybersecurity Risk Escalation Report

**Escalation Reference:** ESC-001  
**Related Finding:** GAP-001  
**Issue:** Former Employee Account Accessing Patient Records  
**Priority:** Critical — Provisional  
**Status:** Escalated — Simulated Scenario  
**Escalation Owner:** Security Incident Response Team (Proposed)

## 1. Executive Summary

During a simulated review of identity and access management controls, activity was identified suggesting that an account belonging to a former employee accessed patient records after their employment ended.

This may represent unauthorised access to sensitive healthcare information and requires urgent investigation by the appropriate security incident response team.

The activity has not been confirmed as malicious, and a personal data breach has not been established.

## 2. Risk Description

The continued availability and apparent use of a former employee's account may expose MediNova to:

- Unauthorised access to sensitive patient information.
- Potential data confidentiality breaches.
- Regulatory and legal consequences.
- Reputational damage.
- Weaknesses in identity lifecycle management.

## 3. Relevant Security Controls

**ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A**

- A.5.16 — Identity Management.
- A.5.18 — Access Rights.
- A.5.24 — Information Security Incident Management Planning and Preparation.
- A.5.26 — Response to Information Security Incidents.

## 4. Initial Assessment

**Likelihood:** To be determined through investigation.

**Potential Impact:** High, subject to confirmation of the information accessed and its sensitivity.

**Escalation Priority:** Critical — Provisional.

**Incident Classification:** Suspected security incident — pending triage.

The escalation priority is based on the potential urgency of unauthorised patient record access and is not a confirmed final risk rating.

## 5. Immediate Escalation Actions

1. Notify the designated security incident response team immediately.
2. Preserve relevant authentication, access and audit logs.
3. Request investigation of the account's activity and employment status.
4. Coordinate appropriate containment through authorised IT and security personnel.
5. Determine whether patient information was accessed, altered or disclosed.
6. Refer potential personal data breach implications to the Data Protection Officer and relevant decision-makers.
7. Document actions, decisions, responsible persons and timestamps.

## 6. Evidence Required

- Employee departure confirmation.
- User account status and access permissions.
- Authentication and sign-in logs.
- Patient record access audit logs.
- Account disablement history.
- Incident investigation findings.
- Relevant HR–IT offboarding records.

All evidence should be handled through approved secure channels.

## 7. Roles and Responsibilities

**Security Incident Response Team:** Investigate, classify, contain and coordinate technical response.

**IT Operations:** Support account access investigation and authorised containment.

**Data Protection Officer:** Assess personal data breach implications and applicable notification requirements.

**GRC Analyst:** Record and track the escalation, coordinate risk reporting and monitor corrective actions.

**Risk Owner / Management:** Make decisions within their delegated authority.

## 8. Regulatory Considerations

If the investigation establishes a personal data breach, the Data Protection Officer and relevant management should assess notification obligations under applicable data protection law, including GDPR Articles 33 and 34 where relevant.

A qualifying supervisory authority notification may be required within 72 hours of becoming aware of a personal data breach, unless the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to individuals' rights and freedoms. Notification to affected individuals depends on the applicable high-risk threshold.

No notification obligation is assumed to have been triggered in this fictional scenario.

- Conduct root cause analysis.

- Verify corrective action effectiveness.
- Obtain authorised closure decisions.
